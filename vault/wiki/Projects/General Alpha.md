# General Alpha

The General Alpha project should initially focus on smaller, flexible power solutions for remote or specialized industrial applications rather than large-scale deployments. Key risks involve high adoption barriers in critical areas and the need for third-party validation to build trust. Next steps involve targeting early adopters and securing phased financing through various partnerships.

## Key points
- Initial product efforts should start with smaller-scale, more flexible solutions like portable or remote power instead of aiming for large MW deployment. — *[[General Alpha Interview Notes]], Product / Market (¶1–13)*
- The technology is best suited for remote power supply, transportable power, and specific industrial applications. — *[[General Alpha Interview Notes]], Product / Market (¶1–13)*
- To build trust, the company should rely on third-party independent laboratory and engineer verification reports. — *[[General Alpha Interview Notes]], Commercialization (¶23–29)*
- The company should target initial outreach toward state governments, universities, and industrial clients who are open to innovation. — *[[General Alpha Interview Notes]], 3. Actionable Next Steps (GTM Strategy) (¶43–47)*
- Financing should be phased, combining VC/PE capital with government grants and corporate R&D partnerships. — *[[General Alpha Interview Notes]], 3. Actionable Next Steps (GTM Strategy) (¶43–47)*
- Customers require clear, quantified advantages in cost, construction timelines, and operational reliability to switch from existing solutions. — *[[General Alpha Interview Notes]], Commercialization (¶23–29)*
