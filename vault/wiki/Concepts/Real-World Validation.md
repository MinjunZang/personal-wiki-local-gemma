# Real-World Validation

Proving technology value requires moving beyond models by validating results through real-world physical experiments and third-party reports. This verification process is necessary because model predictions alone are insufficient for establishing true credibility. The goal is to achieve a continuous alignment between virtual simulations and physical reality.

## Key points
- To build trust, one should rely on third-party independent laboratory and engineer verification reports covering reliability, temperature, and lifespan tests. — *[[General Alpha Interview Notes]], Commercialization (¶23–29)*
- True credibility still depends on verification through real experiments, as model evaluations only show that the model looks good. — *[[Digital Twin Meeting Notes]], 核心规则与主题*
- Reliability, uptime, and lifetime must be demonstrated using real-world pilot operating data. — *[[General Alpha Interview Notes]], 2. Key Risks Identified (¶35–42)*
- The value of digital twins is not just in being able to calculate, but in calculating accurately and being implementable. — *[[Digital Twin Meeting Notes]], 互动与案例分析 > 典型案例*
- In the injection-molding example, simulation results based on DOE data differed substantially from real measurements, which shows why model accuracy and physical calibration matter. — *[[Digital Twin Meeting Notes]], 互动与案例分析 > 典型案例*
