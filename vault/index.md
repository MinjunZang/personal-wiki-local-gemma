# Deep-Tech Customer Discovery Wiki

My notes from customer-discovery work for two deep-tech startup projects: General Alpha (compact energy technology) and DTCS (digital twins for chemistry and manufacturing).

Start here: pick a project or concept, follow its related-note links, and use each note's *Sources* section to open the original evidence in `raw/`.

## Projects
*Named projects and startups*

- [[DTCS]] — DTCS is a digital twin designed for chemical science that integrates computational chemistry, spectroscopy simulations, experimental data, and AI optimization.
- [[General Alpha]] — The General Alpha project should initially focus on smaller, flexible power solutions for remote or specialized industrial applications rather than large-scale deployments.

## Concepts
*Ideas, methods, and market topics*

- [[Chemical R&D Pain Points]] — Hypothesized pain points in chemistry and materials R&D revolve around slow characterization processes, the high cost associated with lab time, and the need for stronger feedback loops between theory and experiments.
- [[Customer Discovery Interviews]] — Customer discovery interviews are designed to learn about a customer's current processes and pain points rather than attempting to sell a product.
- [[Digital Twins in Manufacturing]] — Digital Twins aim to connect virtual models with physical manufacturing processes to solve the issue of siloed experimentation and simulation.
- [[Early Adopter Markets]] — Early adoption efforts should focus on smaller-scale, flexible applications and specific institutional partners who are more willing to test innovative technologies.
- [[Financeable Energy Technology]] — For energy technologies to gain investor and customer backing, they must provide quantified metrics, demonstrate reliability through real-world data, and adopt a phased financing approach.
- [[Real-World Validation]] — Proving technology value requires moving beyond models by validating results through real-world physical experiments and third-party reports.
- [[Target Customer Segments]] — DTCS plans to interview three distinct customer segments to compare pain severity, workflow, and willingness to pay.
