# Financeable Energy Technology

For energy technologies to be considered financeable, investors require transparent, quantified metrics, and demonstrable proof of reliability through real-world data. Early market entry should focus on smaller, flexible applications and target early adopters through phased funding and third-party validation. Key risks include high adoption barriers in critical areas and the need to prove performance against established solutions.

## Key points
- Competing against established solutions requires third-party validated data rather than just concept marketing. — *[[General Alpha Finance Advisor Interview]], 2. Key Risks Identified (¶35–42)*
- Uptime, lifetime, and maintainability must be demonstrated through real-world pilot operating data. — *[[General Alpha Finance Advisor Interview]], 2. Key Risks Identified (¶35–42)*
- Customers need transparent, quantified metrics on $/kWh and build times. — *[[General Alpha Finance Advisor Interview]], 2. Key Risks Identified (¶35–42)*
- Rely on third-party independent laboratory and engineer verification reports to build trust. — *[[General Alpha Finance Advisor Interview]], Commercialization (¶23–29)*
- Small-scale pilot projects partnered with state governments, universities, public entities, or corporate R&D/innovation teams can reduce customer adoption risk. — *[[General Alpha Finance Advisor Interview]], Commercialization (¶23–29)*
- Start with smaller-scale, more flexible solutions rather than pushing for large-scale MW deployment initially. — *[[General Alpha Finance Advisor Interview]], Interviewer / Project: General Alpha (Finance Advisor) + Topic: Customer Interview & Go-To-Market Strategy + 1. - Phased financing can combine VC/PE capital for initial development with government grants and corporate R&D partnerships. — *[[General Alpha Finance Advisor Interview]], 3. Actionable Next Steps (GTM Strategy) (¶43–47)*
